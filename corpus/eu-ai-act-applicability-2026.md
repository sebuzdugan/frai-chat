# EU AI Act — what applies when (status: September 2026)

> Working summary of the application dates in Article 113 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and the
> changes made by the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (published in the Official
> Journal on 24 July 2026). Technical summary, not legal advice. For binding text see
> https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401689

## The dates

- **1 August 2024** — the AI Act entered into force.
- **2 February 2025** — prohibited practices (Article 5) and the AI literacy duty (Article 4) started to apply.
- **2 August 2025** — obligations for general-purpose AI models (Articles 53 and 55), governance bodies, and the penalties regime started to apply.
- **2 August 2026** — the Act became generally applicable, including the **Article 50 transparency obligations**. This date was **not** postponed by the Digital Omnibus.
- **2 December 2026** — end of the grace period for the Article 50(2) marking duty for generative AI systems that were already placed on the market before 2 August 2026.
- **2 December 2027** — high-risk systems listed in **Annex III** (postponed by the Digital Omnibus from 2 August 2026).
- **2 August 2028** — high-risk AI that is a safety component of products covered by **Annex I** (postponed from 2 August 2027).

## What the Digital Omnibus changed, and what it did not

The Digital Omnibus postponed the high-risk timelines (Annex III and Annex I) and adjusted parts of
the compliance machinery. It did **not** postpone the transparency duties in Article 50. A company
deploying a chatbot, an AI agent, synthetic media, or AI-generated public-interest text in the EU is
inside the enforceable period today.

## Article 50 in practice

- **Article 50(1)** — AI systems that interact directly with people must be designed so the person is
  informed they are dealing with an AI, unless that is obvious to a reasonably well-informed person.
  The duty falls on the **provider**. A chat widget answered by an AI agent needs a notice where the
  person meets it, for example in the chat header.
- **Article 50(2)** — providers of systems generating synthetic audio, image, video or text must mark
  outputs in a machine-readable format, detectable as artificially generated or manipulated.
- **Article 50(3)** — deployers of emotion-recognition or biometric-categorisation systems must inform
  the people exposed to them.
- **Article 50(4)** — deployers must disclose deep fakes, and must disclose AI-generated text published
  to inform the public on matters of public interest unless a human reviewed it and someone holds
  editorial responsibility.
- **Article 50(5)** — the information must be given clearly at the latest at the time of the first
  interaction or exposure.

## Penalties (Article 99)

- Breach of the **prohibited practices** in Article 5: up to **€35 million or 7%** of total worldwide
  annual turnover, whichever is higher.
- Breach of most other obligations, **including Article 50**: up to **€15 million or 3%** of turnover.
- Supplying incorrect, incomplete or misleading information to authorities: up to **€7.5 million or 1%**.
- For SMEs and start-ups, the fine is the lower of the percentage and the fixed amount.

## Common questions

**Does a human-answered chat widget need a notice?** No. The duty applies when an AI system interacts
with the person. If a bot or AI agent answers, or answers first, people must be told.

**Is a notice inside the widget enough?** It must be clear and given at the first interaction. A line
in a privacy policy alone is weak evidence; a visible notice where the conversation starts is stronger.

**Does a label in the footer cover AI-generated images?** Article 50(2) asks for machine-readable
marking by the provider of the generating system; Article 50(4) asks deployers to disclose deep fakes.
A human-visible label is what users see, machine-readable marking is what the Act requires of providers.

**Is this only the EU?** Article 50 is the clearest rule, but California's AI Transparency Act and US
FTC enforcement point the same way: tell people when they are dealing with AI.
